## Featured Projects:
<b>Examining the correlation between rent increases, population density, and metropolitan status in selected US cities to predict the rates of unhoused people and families.</b>

Over the last ten years, housing costs in the United States have climbed while salaries have stayed the same or increased slowly (Kemeny and Storker, 2024). As a result, communities have been priced out of their homes or evicted. Our research aims to determine whether a statistical relationship exists between the rate of rent increases and the rates of unhoused people and families in those communities. This paper briefly describes the data science process the Brookings Institute used. Homelessness trends, types, and service delivery ecosystems vary considerably across US cities (Hadden, Loh, and Love, 2024). The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) annually estimates Fair Market Rents (FMRs), providing the maximum allowable rental prices for defined metropolitan areas, some HUD-defined subdivisions, and each non-metropolitan county. The dataset guides the determination of payment standard amounts for the Housing Choice Voucher. This federal program helps low-income families obtain safe, affordable housing in the private market. These values are analyzed through the data science process to establish the minimum affordable rental rates by selected cities in the United States.
