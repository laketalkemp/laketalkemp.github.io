About

LaKeta L. Kemp is a third-year doctoral candidate in Data Science at the Halicioğlu Data Science Institute (HDSI), UC San Diego, where she is a member of the Climate Analytics Lab under the supervision of Dr. Duncan Watson-Parris. Her research sits at the intersection of differentiable climate modeling, machine learning for Earth system science, and the humanitarian dimensions of climate data access.

LaKeta's path to atmospheric science was shaped, in part, by her upbringing in a kinship care household, raised by her grandmother. Her grandmother's perspective on community resilience and resourcefulness became a formative influence on her understanding of what rigorous quantitative work is for, and on her commitment to research that remains consequentially grounded even as it advances technically. A first-generation college student, she earned a Bachelor of Science in Molecular Biosciences from Arizona State University before completing graduate study at Howard University and pursuing doctoral work at UCSD, bringing a foundation in biological systems science to bear on questions of atmospheric physics and climate modeling.

Her current research develops neural network emulators for the atmospheric physics parameterization schemes within JaxGCM, a fully differentiable General Circulation Model, and applies physics-informed super-resolution generative adversarial networks to climate data downscaling for humanitarian applications in displaced population contexts. A parallel strand of applied work addresses probabilistic early warning system calibration for extreme heat and humidity events in Médecins Sans Frontières clinical settings.

LaKeta holds a National GEM Consortium Fellowship, through which she completed a research placement at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory. She is a Fall 2026 Graduate Research Mentorship Program (GRMP) Fellow, supported by the Montgomery Graduate Fellowships Fund, and a Strategic Convene and Influence Award recipient.

Research

Thread I

Differentiable Atmospheric Physics Emulation

LaKeta's primary research in the Climate Analytics Lab concerns the development of neural network emulators for atmospheric physics parameterization schemes within JaxGCM, a fully differentiable General Circulation Model implemented in JAX that combines the Dinosaur dynamical core with the SPEEDY physics parameterization scheme.

The differentiability of JaxGCM's architecture demands more of its emulators than conventional surrogate models require. Where a standard neural emulator need only reproduce a parameterization's output, one integrated into JaxGCM's gradient-based optimization pipeline must also return accurate derivatives with respect to its inputs. LaKeta's emulators accept multiple climate state variables and return both the output fields and their derivatives for the major atmospheric physics parameters, enabling end-to-end gradient-based optimization to propagate through the full GCM pipeline in ways that conventional non-differentiable parameterizations cannot support.

Thread II

Climate Downscaling for Humanitarian Applications

The second thread of LaKeta's research addresses a structural inequity in global climate data infrastructure. ERA5, the fifth-generation ECMWF atmospheric reanalysis product and the de facto global standard for retrospective climate analysis, operates at a spatial resolution that introduces systematic biases in regions with complex local topography and land-surface heterogeneity, conditions disproportionately characteristic of Global South displacement contexts.

Her GRMP fellowship project develops a physics-informed Super-Resolution Generative Adversarial Network (SRGAN) to downscale coarse ERA5 fields to site-specific resolutions suitable for displacement monitoring. The architecture's Thermodynamic Regime Classifier routes inputs through specialized generator sub-networks conditioned on physical state rather than geographic label, with a Wasserstein GAN with Gradient Penalty loss formulation providing training stability and physics-consistency constraints enforcing conservation properties the network cannot reliably infer from data alone.

GRMP Fellowship · Fall 2026

Validating Climate Data to Protect the Rights and Health of Displaced Populations

Montgomery Graduate Fellowships Fund · UCSD GRMP

ERA5 reanalysis data systematically underperforms in the high-heterogeneity environments that characterize displacement sites across the Global South. This project develops a physics-informed SRGAN to downscale coarse ERA5 fields to site-specific resolution suitable for displacement monitoring. A Thermodynamic Regime Classifier partitions inputs by thermodynamic state; WGAN-GP loss stabilizes adversarial training; physics-consistency constraints enforce conservation laws the network cannot reliably infer from data alone.

Strategic Convene and Influence Award · 2026–2027

Displacement Hotspot Vulnerability Mapping Tool

Extreme Weather and Environmental Displacement and Health Initiative

Grounded in frameworks of Recognition Justice and Distributive Justice, this project applies the SRGAN downscaling architecture to the identification of communities with limited adaptive capacity to climate-driven displacement. Downscaled ERA5 fields are integrated with INFORM Risk Index variables to produce tiered vulnerability scores structured to mirror the UNHCR-IDC Vulnerability Screening Tool, designed for use by humanitarian agencies operating in regions where data scarcity compounds existing inequities in climate risk assessment.

Applied Research · Target: November 2026

Probabilistic Early Warning System Calibration for Extreme Heat and Humidity

MSF Spain, Humanitarian Action on Climate and Environment (HACE) Branch

Current operational EWS in MSF clinical contexts carry structural limitations: a 48-hour forecast horizon, deterministic threshold logic anchored at the 95th climatological percentile, and calibration biases from CORDEX projections optimized for Northern Hemisphere domains. This proof of concept proposes a probabilistic conformal prediction layer, a Mixture of Experts thermodynamic routing network, and an Online Conformal Prediction bias correction module that updates CORDEX projections against field sensor telemetry without requiring full model retraining.
